# Running Comeback — on-ramp starting 2026-09-02

> **Status: active.** Conditioning that runs *alongside* the strength blocks, not inside them.
> Written 2026-08-30 at the athlete's ask ("where should I start?"). This file is binding on
> block design the same way `active-issues.md` is: **B6 onward must read it before scheduling
> axial progression.** See *Interaction with the strength block*.

## Background

Ran regularly ~4–5 years ago, topping out around 10k — his own words: *"it was very hard."*
Five years off. Original on-ramp, as he recalls it: 1 min run / 1 min walk alternating for
20–30 min, lengthening the run and later shortening the walk. **That recollection is right and
the method is sound** — it's essentially Couch-to-5k. No running or cardio history exists
anywhere else in this repo; this file starts the record.

## The two things that decide this plan

1. **The limiter is connective tissue, not the aerobic system.** He trains 4×/week on 60–75 s
   rest. Aerobic capacity returns in ~3 weeks; Achilles, plantar fascia, tibia and patellar
   tendon take 3–6 months to re-adapt to impact, and they give no warning until they fail. The
   textbook 37-year-old comeback failure is *week 3 felt easy → ran 5k → tendinopathy at week 6*.
2. **That failure mode is specifically his.** B4 W1 sumo: planned 345, pulled **425**. Squat
   backoffs at **@8.5** under a cap written to stop exactly that. `clinical-override` already
   records the conclusion — *"train light" is not a restriction for this athlete; write the
   numbers down.* Same treatment here: the dose is on the page, and the lever is **running
   minutes per week**, not how it feels.

Also relevant: 180 lb at 38 is meaningful load per footstrike, and the lumbar spine is under
active trigger-point treatment.

## Timing — why now, and the caveat

**Now is the best window available**, and waiting is the worse option:

- Legs are the least loaded they've been in years — squat 225 flat, sumo 225 flat, both loaded
  hinges cut, zero real axial work. Interference cost today is near zero.
- Bench, pull-up and dip are limited by the **shoulder**. Running does not touch it.
- **B6 (mid-Sep) is where squat and sumo return on clearance.** Starting running then would put
  the fragile weeks of the on-ramp (3–6) directly on top of an axial return. Starting now gets
  them done while the barbell work is trivial.

⚠️ **Caveat, stated plainly:** running week 1 sits inside the doctor's no-heavy window
(→ ~2026-09-04). Twenty minutes of jog/walk is not heavy lifting by any reading, and graded
aerobic activity is first-line for non-specific low back pain — mainstream, not contrarian. But
this is the **third** activity the clinic doesn't know about (see `active-issues.md` → the
"shocked" pattern). **Ask at the ~2026-09-11 visit.** It is the easiest of the outstanding
disclosure items because the answer is almost certainly yes.

## The plan — Wed + Sun, 8 weeks

**Two days a week. Not three.**

C25K prescribes three; he is running alongside a 4-day powerlifting week and a treated lumbar
spine, and two well-spaced impact days is the version that costs nothing in the gym. Frequency
goes up only if running becomes a stated priority — see *Open questions*.

**Day choice:**
- **Wed** — the only true mid-week gap. Sits after D2 (pull-up, no legs) and before D3 (dip, no
  legs). Zero interference on either side. The best slot in the week.
- **Sun** — sits before D1 Mon, which is a 225 squat and a bench session. Neither is touched.
- **Sat is deliberately NOT used.** Friday's Bulgarian Split Squat is the block's DOMS generator;
  Saturday running lands on sore legs. It is also the day D4 slips to when it slips.
- **Never on a lifting day.** He trains 6–7am; a second session will not happen.

## Standing orders

- **Conversational pace, every interval, every week, including week 8.** If you can't speak a
  full sentence, you're running too fast. This is the most-violated rule in any comeback and the
  one this athlete is most likely to break.
- **Treadmill for the first four weeks** where there's a choice. Lower impact per step while the
  tissue re-adapts, and it enforces the pace rule *mechanically* — speed is set, not drifted up.
  Given the documented tendency, that's a feature, not a compromise.
- **New shoes before Sep 2.** Five-year-old midsole foam is dead regardless of mileage. Cheapest
  injury prevention available and the item everyone skips.
- **Nothing is added because it felt easy.** Not a minute, not a rep, not a third day. It *will*
  feel easy — that's the engine reporting, and the engine is not what breaks.
- **Two-week holds. No jumping ahead a block.** Same rule as accessories.
- **Log the back check on run days too** → `brain/back-checks.md`. Repetitive axial impact on a
  spine under treatment is a real stimulus, and the anesthetic argument applies unchanged: the
  in-session signal is chemically muffled, so the morning after is the only honest read.

### Hard stops — running stops, not reduces

Any one of these: stop running and tell the coach; the leg-symptom line also goes to the clinic.

- New or worsening **leg symptoms below the knee** (pain, numbness, tingling, weakness) — already
  an escalation trigger in `active-issues.md`
- Back check reads **`sore` after two runs in a row**
- **Shin pain that persists past the warm-up** (tibial stress — the classic comeback injury)
- **Achilles pain first thing in the morning** (the tendinopathy tell)
- **Any pain that changes your gait**

## Interaction with the strength block

Stated explicitly, per CLAUDE.md's overlap requirement:

| Pair | Read |
|---|---|
| Running × **Bench / Pull-up / Dip** | **No cost.** Different tissue, different limiter (shoulder), different days. The strive plan is untouched. |
| Running × **Squat / Sumo @ 225 @6** | **No meaningful cost** while the medical override holds — these are technique work. |
| Running × **Wednesday rest** | Real but small. Wed was the only mid-week rest day. If D3 Thursday runs flat **two weeks running**, the Wednesday run shrinks — the dip does not. |
| Running × **Seated Calf Raise (D4)** | Now **load-bearing**. It entered B5 as a coverage hole-filler; it is Achilles/calf preparation for running now. **Keep it in B6.** |

### 🔴 The collision to plan for: B6

Running weeks **2–7 sit inside B6**, and B6 is where squat and sumo come back on clearance. Two
progressions on the same tissue at the same time is how this goes wrong.

> **Rule for B6 onward: when squat or sumo steps up, the running holds flat that week.**
> Never progress axial load and running volume in the same week. If they collide, the barbell
> wins — running is the newer, more fragile adaptation and it can wait a week without cost.

## Timeline — the honest version

- Continuous **20–25 min** (roughly a 5k) is realistic around **week 10–12**.
- **A 10k that isn't miserable is a 6–9 month project**, not a two-month one. Worth noting that
  last time he reached 10k he described it as *"very hard"* — which suggests he arrived there
  faster than his tissue did. Not repeating that is the point of the eight weeks above.
