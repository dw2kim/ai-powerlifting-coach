## Background

## The two things that decide this plan

1. **The limiter is connective tissue, not the aerobic system.** He trains 4×/week on 60–75 s
   rest. Aerobic capacity returns in ~3 weeks; Achilles, plantar fascia, tibia and patellar
   tendon take 3–6 months to re-adapt to impact, and they give no warning until they fail. The
   textbook 37-year-old comeback failure is *week 3 felt easy → ran 5k → tendinopathy at week 6*.
2. **That failure mode is specifically his.** B4 W1 sumo: planned 345, pulled **425**. Squat
   backoffs at **@8.5** under a cap written to stop exactly that. `clinical-override` already
   records the conclusion — *"train light" is not a restriction for this athlete; write the
   numbers down.* Same treatment here: the dose is on the page, and the lever is **running
   minutes per week**, not how it feels.

**Session 1 confirmed point 1 on day one.** 10 minutes of running produced a next-morning knee
symptom. That is a very low dose to provoke anything, and it is the single best argument against
the frequency ramp he wants. It is data, not a scare.

Also relevant: 180 lb at 38 is meaningful load per footstrike, and the lumbar spine is under
active trigger-point treatment.

- **B6 (mid-Sep) is where squat and sumo return on clearance.** Starting running then would put

- **On the other five days, the walk continues.** It's low-impact aerobic base and it's good for
  the back. Do not delete it in favour of more running.

## Standing orders

- **Conversational pace, every interval, every step.** If you can't speak a full sentence, you're
  running too fast. The most-violated rule in any comeback and the one this athlete will break.
- **Quicker, shorter steps. Land under the hips, not out in front.** Overstriding is the primary
  mechanical driver of knee load in returning runners, and raising cadence cuts peak
  patellofemoral force with no other change. **This is the highest-yield cue available** given a
  465 lb squat attached to zero impact conditioning — the strength is fine, the shock absorption
  is what's missing.
- **Avoid cambered surfaces** — a sloped road shoulder reliably produces knee and ITB trouble.
- **Treadmill where there's a choice**, especially early. Lower impact per step, flat, and it
  enforces the pace rule *mechanically* — speed is set, not drifted up. Given the documented
  tendency, that's a feature.
- **New shoes.** Five-year-old midsole foam is dead regardless of mileage. Cheapest injury
  prevention available and the item everyone skips.
- **Nothing is added because it felt easy.** Not a minute, not a rep, not a day. It *will* feel
  easy — that's the engine reporting, and the engine is not what breaks.
- **Steps are earned by two clean mornings, never by the calendar.**
- **Log the back check on run days too** → `brain/back-checks.md`. Repetitive axial impact on a
  spine under treatment is real loading. The knee goes in the session log below.

## Frequency roadmap — the answer to "can I run almost every day?"

**Yes eventually. No now, and not soon.**

**Running does not wreck knees; at recreational volumes it protects them.** Alentorn-Geli et al.
(2017, *JOSPT*), meta-analysis of ~114,000 people: hip/knee osteoarthritis prevalence **3.5% in
recreational runners · 10.2% in sedentary non-runners · 13.3% in competitive/elite runners.** A
U-shaped curve — recreational running beats *not running* for joint health, because cartilage is
load-adapted tissue that needs cyclic loading. The folk belief is backwards at this volume.

**What actually injures returning runners is rate of change, not frequency.** Novice and
returning runners carry the highest injury rates, concentrated in the first months, and the
mechanism is the ramp. Bone remodeling after impact passes through a resorption phase before new
bone is laid down — which is why stress reactions cluster in build-ups. Tendon collagen synthesis
peaks roughly 24–72 h post-loading. **Impact on consecutive days before that window closes is the
problem** — which is why habitual daily runners are fine (built over years) and a returning runner
going daily in month one is not.

| Phase | Days/wk | Roughly | Gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Now** | **2** | → ~late Oct | — |
| Next | 3 | ~month 3 | On-ramp finished, no knee/shin/Achilles symptoms for 3 weeks |
| Then | 4 | ~month 5–6 | Same, plus lifting unaffected |
| Maybe | 5 | month 9+ | Same. **Do not plan past this.** |

**The cap, and why.** He is a lifter adding running, not a runner adding lifting: two
partial-thickness shoulder tears, a lumbar spine under trigger-point injections, and four barbell
sessions a week that are the priority. Running seven days deletes every rest day he owns, and the
Big 5 pay that bill first. **Long-term ceiling: 4–5 easy short days, not 7.**
